The Sticker Weekly
Week of May 18, 2026

The week arrived with a DTC reckoning and an AI gold rush running in parallel: Everlane sold to Shein for a fraction of its peak valuation — buried in debt, with common shareholders walking away empty — while TikTok-native brands like 4AM Skin are landing 1,700-store national retail deals in their first year. Meanwhile, Google embedded agentic AI into three billion Android devices, and consumer brands from Hims & Hers to ecommerce platforms are racing to put AI at the center of the customer journey. The message is stark: DTC brands that will matter in five years are either AI-native or capital-efficient and category-specific — there is no comfortable middle.
 
🔁 M&A
Mergers & Acquisitions
🛍 Shein Buys Everlane in $100M Debt Reckoning
  • Shein acquired San Francisco-based DTC fashion brand Everlane for $100M — a fraction of the $1.7B valuation it commanded at its 2021 peak. The board approved the deal on May 16; common stockholders receive nothing from the transaction.
  • Everlane entered the sale carrying ~$90M in debt: a $65M revolving credit facility from CIT Northbridge and a $25M term loan from restructuring specialist Gordon Brothers. L Catterton, the brand's majority owner, drove the exit process after publicly seeking new capital or a strategic partner in March 2026.
  • For Shein, it is a brand equity grab: Everlane's "radical transparency" positioning, loyal 2M+ customer file, and US DTC infrastructure give the Chinese fast-fashion giant a premium American identity it could not have built organically — acquired at essentially the cost of Everlane's debt load.
  • What to watch: whether Shein runs Everlane independently (the LVMH multi-brand playbook) or cannibalizes it for supply chain insight and customer data, quietly sunsetting the mission-driven brand.
Trend: Legacy DTC brands built on mission-driven differentiation are trading at distressed valuations when execution falters — turning brand equity into cheap acquisition currency for deep-pocketed incumbents.
Bloomberg
🛒 YC Alum Skio Exits for $105M Cash on Just $8M Raised #news-searcher
  • Skio, a DTC subscription commerce platform and Y Combinator alum, was acquired by competitor Recharge for $105M in an all-cash deal — having raised only $8M total since founding. Founder Kennan Frost and CEO Aidan Thibodeaux built it with near-zero marketing spend.
  • At exit, Skio had $32M in ARR and had processed $4B in cumulative payments for DTC brands — a 3.3x revenue multiple that validates the thesis that DTC subscription infrastructure generates durable, high-margin recurring revenue at premium exit multiples.
  • The combined Recharge + Skio platform becomes the dominant subscription commerce layer for DTC brands, locking in recurring revenue management for thousands of operators across beauty, food, wellness, and apparel.
  • The lesson for founders: the most durable ecommerce businesses are infrastructure plays, not brand plays — subscription management compounds network effects in ways that inventory-heavy DTC brands structurally cannot.
Trend: The best risk-adjusted returns in consumer ecommerce are coming from capital-efficient infrastructure layers — not the DTC brands themselves — as subscription and retention tooling becomes essential operating infrastructure for every brand.
TechCrunch
🥞 Babybel Parent Acquires Brainiac in Better-For-You Pivot #news-searcher
  • Bel Group — the French dairy company behind Babybel, The Laughing Cow, and GoGo squeeZ — acquired Ingenuity Foods, maker of Brainiac functional fruit snacks fortified with omega-3s and choline for kids. Brainiac posted triple-digit revenue growth in the year prior to the acquisition.
  • Incoming Bel North America CEO Peter McGuinness (former Chobani president) explicitly signaled this is the first in a pipeline of acquisitions as Bel repositions its North American portfolio toward functional, better-for-you formats targeting modern wellness-conscious families.
  • Brainiac followed the DTC-to-retail playbook precisely: viral social proof, national retailer velocity, function-forward positioning, and triple-digit growth — then a CPG acquisition. The deal validates "supplement-in-disguise" functional snacks as a genuine strategic M&A target for legacy food companies.
  • McGuinness's presence is the tell: his Chobani playbook — premiumize a commodity category on nutrition claims, scale through club and mass retail — is exactly what Bel intends to execute across its North America portfolio, starting with Brainiac.
Trend: Established CPG giants are using M&A as an R&D substitute — acquiring culturally resonant better-for-you DTC brands rather than developing functional product lines internally from scratch.

🤖 CONSUMER AI
Consumer AI Spotlight
📱 Google Turns Android Into an Agentic AI Platform
  • At its Android Show on May 12, Google unveiled Gemini Intelligence — agentic AI baked into the world's largest mobile OS. The flagship demo: AI copying a grocery list from Notes and automatically adding items to a shopping cart in a retailer's app, without any mid-flow user intervention.
  • "Create My Widget" lets any user describe a home-screen widget in natural language and Gemini builds and deploys it instantly — democratizing custom UI across 3 billion Android devices. Rambler adds AI-powered dictation to Gboard with filler-word removal, mid-sentence corrections, and multilingual code-switching.
  • Rollout begins with Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel this summer and expands to all Android devices by end of 2026 — the largest capability jump for Android since Google Play launched, landing directly inside the consumer commerce and discovery funnel.
  • Strategic implication for consumer apps: every product that relied on owning a UI step in a multi-step user journey now competes with Gemini completing that step in the background. The apps that survive will be those with irreplaceable proprietary data or identity.
Trend: The OS layer is becoming the dominant consumer AI surface — fragmenting the user journey away from individual apps toward ambient, system-level intelligence that completes tasks across the entire device ecosystem.
TechCrunch
🧠 IronSource Founders Raise $60M for Agentic DTC OS, ZyG
  • ZyG — built by the founders of ironSource, which went public at an $11B NYSE valuation before merging with Unity — raised a $60M Series A led by Accel at a $500M valuation, two months after emerging from stealth with a $58M seed round. The company raised $118M in 90 days.
  • ZyG OS is an end-to-end agentic AI platform for DTC brands: it autonomously identifies products with scale potential, then handles brand building, creative production, user acquisition, conversion optimization, and logistics — collapsing a full DTC marketing-and-ops team into a software layer.
  • Accel's Sonali De Rycker is joining the board; co-investors include Bessemer Venture Partners, Lightspeed, and Felix Capital. The bet: as agentic AI collapses the cost of brand launches, the platform enabling them will compound network effects across thousands of DTC operators.
  • The IronSource pedigree matters — they built growth infrastructure for the mobile app era and scaled it to an $11B exit. ZyG is the same founders rebuilding that infrastructure for the AI-native DTC era, with a deeper product surface and a defensible data moat.
Trend: Agentic AI is collapsing the cost of DTC brand operations — enabling a new model where launching and scaling a consumer brand becomes an AI-automated workflow rather than a headcount-heavy, capital-intensive build.
The Next Web
🏋 Hims & Hers Builds AI Companion for GLP-1 Weight-Loss Journeys #news-searcher
  • Hims & Hers ($608M in Q1 2026 revenue, +4% YoY) is imminently launching an AI companion built for patients on GLP-1 weight-loss journeys — covering medication adherence, side-effect navigation, nutritional adaptation, and behavioral coaching across the full treatment arc.
  • The company has assembled a 40-person dedicated AI team and is making a strategic pivot from prescription fulfillment platform to full-stack AI-native healthcare companion — competing for the behavioral change journey that sits beyond the prescription itself.
  • The timing is deliberate: as GLP-1 drugs move toward generic status and compounded alternatives face regulatory pressure, Hims & Hers is building a sticky AI layer that retains patients regardless of which drug they're on or where they fill it. The companion is the durable moat.
  • Tens of millions of patients are seeking digital behavioral support as part of a fundamentally new kind of health journey — making this the first mass-market health behavior where persistent AI coaching is clinically essential, not just a nice-to-have feature.
Trend: GLP-1 drugs are creating the defining consumer AI wedge in healthcare — the first mass-market therapeutic behavior where AI coaching is structurally necessary, building a retention moat for whoever owns the full journey.

Funding Rounds
💄 TikTok Darling 4AM Skin Closes $4M Seed, Lands 1,745 Target Stores #things-to-read
  • 4AM closed an oversubscribed $4M+ seed round led by CAVU Consumer Partners — early backers of Poppi ($1.85B Pepsi exit), Nécessaire, and Vital Proteins — and simultaneously launched its hero product, Clean Sheets facial wipes, in 1,745 Target stores nationwide.
  • The brand posted 4x revenue growth year-over-year, sold through four production runs, and reached the #4 cleanser position on TikTok Shop. Founder Jade Beguelin (former Wall Street derivatives trader) has built a 150,000-follower TikTok audience documenting the brand's journey in real time.
  • Clean Sheets are credit-card-sized individually-wrapped wipes delivering serum-grade actives — centella asiatica, vitamin B5, glycerin — positioned as a single-step cleanser, toner, and treatment. Distributed simultaneously across Target, Revolve, Bloomingdale's, and C.O. Bigelow.
  • CAVU's involvement is the strongest signal: the firm backed Poppi's $3M seed and rode it to a $1.85B Pepsi acquisition. 4AM is following the identical playbook — TikTok virality → national retail velocity → CPG strategic acquisition target.
Trend: TikTok has replaced trade shows as the primary DTC brand incubator — compressing the prove-it cycle from years to months and putting category-defining founders directly in front of national retail buyers on day one.
GlobeNewswire
🌊 Functional Hydration Brand waterdrop Closes €100M+ Round #news-searcher
  • Vienna-based waterdrop — maker of sugar-free microdrink cubes and functional hydration sticks — raised over €100M from Aspeya (Philip Morris International's wellness investment arm), Atlantic Grupa, and Olympic ski champion Aleksander Aamodt Kilde. Revenue is ~€150M with 5M+ online customers globally and 50+ physical stores.
  • The PMI/Aspeya involvement is the headline: a tobacco conglomerate deploying capital into functional hydration signals both sector heat and a strategic need — large FMCG incumbents are buying access to wellness-oriented DTC brands they cannot build organically at this growth rate.
  • waterdrop combines DTC-native online scale (5M+ customers) with 50+ physical retail stores — a rare omnichannel position that most DTC brands fail to achieve. Its zero-sugar, ultra-compact format sits directly in the demand wave driving Poppi, Olipop, and the broader better-beverage boom.
  • With €100M+ fresh and a strategic backer in PMI, waterdrop has the firepower to accelerate its US market entry — entering a battleground that has already produced multiple billion-dollar outcomes in the functional beverage category this decade.
Trend: Functional hydration is the new center of gravity for premium consumer wellness investment, attracting non-traditional corporate acquirers seeking exposure to health-oriented consumer behavior through DTC-native brands with proven international scale.
Food Dive
🥤 Cold-Pressed Juice Pioneer Suja Life Raises $200M in IPO
  • Suja Life — the cold-pressed juice brand that also owns Slice Soda and Vive Organic — raised nearly $200M in its public debut, pricing shares at $21. Revenue grew 26% year-over-year heading into the offering, reflecting strong consumer momentum in the functional beverage category.
  • Shares fell 14% on the first day of trading — a reminder that public market investors grade consumer brands on a clear path to profitability, not growth rates alone, even in a booming category. Revenue growth without visible margin discipline is no longer a public market story.
  • Suja's multi-brand portfolio strategy — cold-pressed juice, functional soda, and superfood shots under one parent — mirrors the house-of-brands playbook gaining traction in consumer: prove core brand velocity, then acquire or incubate in adjacent categories to amortize infrastructure.
  • The IPO provides listed equity as acquisition currency, putting Suja in direct competition with well-capitalized rivals as functional beverages heat up against Olipop, Poppi, and fast-growing international entrants like waterdrop.
Trend: The better-for-you beverage category is maturing toward public markets — but investors are grading consumer brands on profitability discipline, forcing DTC-native brands to balance growth investment with margin expansion earlier than any previous generation.
